Problem and users

Not all fixed issues are truly resolved.

A corrective action can be completed while its effect on workers remains problematic. Remedy Recheck keeps those two facts separate so the next decision is not lost.

How it works

From resolution to verification

Resolving an issue means documenting the solution and checking that it works in practice. Remedy Recheck helps bridge that gap, alongside audits, worker feedback, and human judgement.

Step 01 · Record the Remedy

Document the suggested solution.

Remedy Recheck keeps the issue, agreed solution, responsible party, and supporting evidence in one place. Creating a clear record before checking whether the solution has worked.

✓ Demonstrated in the working application

A focused reviewer workspace

  • Protected sign-in and a session-based reviewer identity
  • Due-date ordering, search, filters, and outcome rechecks
  • Next actions, owners, due dates, and dated audit history
  • Persistent case updates and object-oriented components

→ Required for IKEA deployment

Organization-level protection

  • IKEA identity integration and server-side role permissions
  • Encrypted transfer and storage in a protected backend
  • Separated worker identities with controlled retention and deletion
  • Central access logs, backup, recovery, and governance

the 5 step process

One solution does not close a case.

Only a recheck that says the situation improved ends the case. Everything else stays open, which is what closes the loop a supplier audit can leave open.

  1. 01

    01 Issue

    An issue is brought up, anonymously

    A supplier issue is reported and logged, and stays open until something proves it ended.
  2. 02

    02 Remedy

    The supplier fixes it

    What the supplier did is recorded. But a fix isn't the same thing as a solved issue.
  3. 03

    03 Recheck

    Workers report back, safely and anonymously

    A while after the fix, workers submit whether the situation improved. Anonymous, so saying the truth is safe.
  4. 04

    04 Result

    Fixed, or not fixed

    Where the answer is no, the result is the evidence, and IKEA can put preassure on the supplier.
  5. 05

    05 Follow-up

    A new fix is asked for, and it is checked again

    The case stays open until an answer says it improved. Then the loop repeats from step 02.

the loop

Open cases stay on the list.

An issue is logged, the supplier fixes it, and workers later report back safely and anonymously. Where the answer is no, the result goes back to the supplier as evidence that the fix didn't work, and a new one is asked for and checked again.

Sustainability

What could this change for workers, and what does it cost?

We went through the five dimensions of the Sustainability Awareness Framework (SusAF) from the course. The social side matters most to us, since the point of the tool is that workers' conditions actually get better. But we tried to look honestly at the other four too.

Social

Workers' problems don't get closed too early

A case isn't done just because the supplier says it fixed something. If conditions haven't changed for the workers, the case stays open. The feedback routes are set up so that speaking up doesn't put workers at risk of retaliation.

Individual

Workers can stay anonymous

Workers give feedback through protected channels, and their names never show up in the case overview that reviewers like Maria work from.

Economic

Time goes to the cases that need it

Every case has an owner and a due date, so follow-up effort goes to cases that are still open rather than ones that are already sorted.

Environmental

Keeping the footprint small

It runs in a normal browser on computers IKEA already has. We only keep the evidence that's needed, and it gets deleted after a set retention period.

Technical

It has to be looked after

If IKEA used it for real, it would need secure login, reliable data, backups, someone responsible for maintaining it, and a plan for when it's eventually shut down.

First, second and third order effects

Both the good and the bad at each level.

First order

Direct effects

Building, hosting, using and eventually shutting down Remedy Recheck all take energy, hardware, maintenance and people's time.

Second order

Effects of using it

Maria notices unresolved cases earlier, can give each one an owner, and follows up before a case gets forgotten.

Third order

Long-term effects

Over time it could change what suppliers and IKEA count as a finished remedy, and give workers more of a say in that.

How would we know it's working?

IKEA's Challenge 4 is about finding high-risk suppliers, keeping track of worker conditions, and measuring whether social impact improves. These are the three things we'd look at.

  1. 1.
    Is the follow-up happening?

    Overdue actions, completed fixes, outcome checks, repeat issues, and how long follow-up takes.

  2. 2.
    Did things get better for the workers?

    Whether conditions improved, and whether workers understood the feedback route and felt safe using it.

  3. 3.
    Is the workers' data protected?

    As little personal data as possible, limited access, logged changes, retention rules, and retaliation concerns handed to people qualified to deal with them.
