The problem

“Fix made” does not always mean “issue solved.”

IKEA needs visibility into labour conditions, supplier risks, and improvement opportunities in Poland. A corrective action can be completed while its effect on workers remains unresolved or unclear.

The solution

One clear path from action to outcome.

RR is a proposed web tool that adds a visible outcome checkpoint to supplier follow-up without replacing audits, complaint channels, or human judgement.

Step 01 · Record the fix

Implementation stays visible, but it is not treated as the final result.

RR records the issue category, agreed action, responsible owner, completion date, and supporting evidence for each supplier case.

Software development

A focused decision-support tool.

The capstone asks for working functionality, clear user value, and maintainable code. RR focuses on one core interaction: turning a completed corrective action into a reviewed outcome and next decision.

⌘

Working implementation

  • Reusable case, outcome, and team components
  • Project information and web app on one host

Data protection

Protect people, not only records

  • Pseudonymous case IDs in reviewer screens
  • Role-based access and an audit trail
  • Worker identity stored separately from case evidence

Data-driven decisions

Information linked to action

  • Issue, fix, outcome, owner, and due date

User-centered design process

Research, prototype, test, and improve.

The course treats design thinking as a non-linear process. Findings from users and stakeholders can move the team back to an earlier stage at any time.

◎

01 · Empathize

Understand the people and context

  • Primary user: Maria, Sustainability & Compliance Manager
  • Include supplier leads, workers, and representatives
  • Study current follow-up decisions and information needs
⌁

02 · Define

Frame the decision problem

  • Completed action and improved outcome are different states
  • Maria needs visibility across Polish supplier cases
  • The problem is follow-up clarity, not a new complaint channel
◉

03 · Ideate

Compare possible responses

  • Explore digital and non-digital alternatives
  • Select the smallest useful feature
  • Keep worker safety and organizational value visible
↗

04 · Prototype

Make the recheck tangible

  • Open a supplier case and review the corrective action
  • Record the outcome and its evidence
  • Show the next owner and due date
↻

05 · Test

Observe use and iterate

  • Test whether users distinguish implementation from outcome
  • Check whether privacy controls support safe participation
  • Revise the problem, workflow, or feature when evidence requires it

Research and feedback

Each test must inform a decision.

The team records whose experience was studied, what assumption was tested, what was observed, and whether the result means continue, revise, investigate further, or stop.

Non-negotiable safeguards

Human judgement stays in the process

  • 01 No automatic case closure
  • 02 Role-based access with sign-in and audit history
  • 03 Worker identities kept outside the reviewer overview
  • 04 Encryption in transit and at rest
  • 05 Retention and deletion dates for every case
  • 06 “Not safely verified” remains a valid outcome

Sustainability

Social value first, with the full footprint in view.

The Sustainability Awareness Framework asks the team to examine individual, social, economic, environmental, and technical effects together.

Social

Support fair treatment and worker voice

Unresolved outcomes remain visible, while feedback routes are designed to reduce retaliation risk.

Individual

Protect agency, safety, and privacy

Workers can contribute to an outcome check without having their identity displayed in the case overview.

Economic

Focus resources on unresolved cases

Clear ownership and due dates reduce repeated follow-up and help teams direct effort where it is needed.

Environmental

Limit the ICT footprint

The service uses existing devices, restrained storage, and defined retention periods for case evidence.

Technical

Keep the system secure and maintainable

Access control, audit history, data quality checks, backups, and deletion rules support long-term use.

Orders of effects

From the software itself to wider change.

First order

Immediate effects

Building, hosting, storing, using, and eventually retiring RR require energy, equipment, maintenance, and staff time.

Second order

Enabling effects

During use, Maria can identify unresolved outcomes sooner, assign ownership, and follow up before a case disappears from view.

Third order

Structural effects

Repeated use can change expectations about supplier accountability, worker participation, and what counts as a completed remedy.

Measuring improvement

From supplier risk to measurable social impact.

Challenge 4 asks IKEA to identify high-risk suppliers, monitor worker conditions, and measure social impact improvements. RR connects those goals to follow-up decisions.

  1. 01
    Monitor the process

    Track overdue actions, completed fixes, outcome checks, repeat issues, and time to follow-up.

  2. 02
    Monitor the human outcome

    Record whether conditions improved and whether the feedback route was understood, accessible, and safe.

  3. 03
    Protect the people behind the data

    Minimize personal data, restrict access, log changes, set retention periods, and escalate retaliation concerns through qualified channels.
